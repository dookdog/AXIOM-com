About

Search, from first principles.

Axiom exists because finding an answer on the modern web started to feel like winning an argument with a vending machine. It shouldn’t. So we went back to the axioms and derived a search engine.

The name is the spec

In mathematics, an axiom is a statement accepted as self-evidently true — the small set of foundations from which everything else is proved. You don’t argue with axioms; you build on them.

We run the company the same way. Five axioms — they fit on one screen — and a standing rule: every feature must be derivable from them. When a roadmap idea contradicts an axiom, the idea loses, no matter how much money it would make. That rule has already killed three “great” ideas. It will kill more. Good.

What went wrong with search

Somewhere in the last two decades, the search box quietly changed sides. Behind most queries today sits an auction: advertisers bidding on your attention, results shaped by what pays rather than what answers, and a tracking apparatus taking notes on every click so tomorrow’s auction clears higher.

None of that is a law of nature. It’s just a business model — one particular answer to the question “who is the customer?” We think the person typing the question should be the customer. Everything about Axiom follows from that single change of variable.

What Axiom is

Axiom is a search engine that treats your query as a question to answer, not an intent to monetize. It reads the web, ranks what it finds on merit, and puts the answer — with its sources — at the top. No sponsored anything. No profile of you accumulating in the background. A results page light enough to feel instant on a mid-range phone.

It’s currently in private beta while we finish the public index. The demo on the home page shows the spirit of the thing; the launch will happen right there.

The mark

Our logo is the letter A reduced to what it can’t live without: a peak and a point. The peak is the climb — the question. The point is where you land — the answer. That’s the whole product in two shapes, which felt appropriately axiomatic. (The dot on the i of the wordmark is the same point, making its second appearance.)

We drew it ourselves from circles and lines, and it ships as a few hundred bytes of SVG. Even the logo is on a performance budget.
